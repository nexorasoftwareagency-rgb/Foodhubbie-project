KDS questions, answered plainly

Do we need to buy a special screen?

No. The kitchen display runs in a browser on any tablet, laptop or TV — there is no proprietary terminal to purchase.

Can the kitchen tell delivery orders apart from dine-in?

Yes. Every ticket carries its channel and destination, so the kitchen knows whether it is going to a table, the counter or a rider.

What is a kitchen display system (KDS)?

A kitchen display system is a screen in the kitchen that replaces paper order tickets. Every order — dine-in, QR table and delivery — appears on it the moment it's placed, with the table number and exact customizations the customer chose. Kitchen staff mark each order cooking / ready / served, and everyone downstream sees the update instantly.

How much does a kitchen display system cost?

FoodHubbie's KDS is included in the standard plan: a flat ₹1–5 per order plus a one-time ₹500 setup. No separate KDS licence, no extra screen subscription — and the free trial covers it.

Does the owner need to be at the restaurant to see orders?

No. The admin dashboard mirrors the kitchen screen from anywhere. Ordering, cooking and delivery statuses are live in your pocket — that's what the same live-orders feed powers.

What happens when a customer customizes an order from the QR menu?

The customization travels with the order to the kitchen screen exactly as chosen — variant, toppings, quantity, notes. Nothing is retyped or summarized, so the cook makes what the customer actually picked.

Do I need special hardware for KDS?

No. Any phone, tablet or old laptop the kitchen already owns runs it in the browser. No dedicated touchscreen terminals or installation visits.

KOT vs KDS: what actually changes

A KOT (kitchen order ticket) is a piece of paper that carries an order to the kitchen. A KDS (kitchen display system) is a screen that carries the same order plus its live status, its channel and its table. The paper is not the problem — the shouting, lost slips and "did anyone start table 6?" are.
