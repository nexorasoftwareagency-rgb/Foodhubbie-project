A stock sheet that updates itself

Most restaurants track stock in a notebook or a spreadsheet that is accurate on Monday and fiction by Friday. Here the count lives next to the order flow: every QR table order, counter bill and delivery order reduces the linked inventory item, so the number you look at is the number after today's service.

What it does on a normal day

📉
Deducts on every order

Dishes are linked to stock items, so a sale reduces the count without anyone touching a spreadsheet. Delivery and dine-in are counted the same way.

🔔
Warns before you run out

Each item has a minimum. When stock reaches it the item is marked low; close to it, it reads reorder-soon — so the purchase list is written before the rush, not during it.

🚫
Sold out means unorderable

Switch a dish's availability off and it stops appearing as orderable on the QR menu. No "sorry, that's finished" conversations from the kitchen to the counter.

📥
Your existing sheet imports

Bring your current list in as CSV — name, SKU, stock, minimum, unit, supplier, cost — up to 500 rows at a time. You do not re-type last month's count.

💰
Cost per item, not per guess

Each item carries its unit cost, which is what turns "we sold ₹40,000" into "we sold ₹40,000 and spent this much on stock".

🧾
Every change is logged

Manual adjustments and low-stock crossings go into the audit trail, with who changed what — useful when the count and the shelf disagree.

Inventory questions, answered plainly

Does stock deduct automatically when an order is placed?

Yes. When a dish is linked to an inventory item, placing an order reduces that item by the quantity sold — from QR table orders, POS bills and delivery orders alike. If no link exists, the system matches the inventory item by name.

Do I need a barcode scanner or special hardware?

No. Stock counts are adjusted in the dashboard on any phone, tablet or laptop, and an existing stock sheet can be imported as CSV. There is no scanner or proprietary hardware to buy.

Can I import the stock sheet I already keep in Excel?

Yes. Export it as CSV with the columns name, sku, stock, threshold, unit, supplier and cost, then import it — up to 500 rows per import. Units, suppliers and per-item cost come in with the stock.

What happens when an item falls below its minimum?

The item shows a low-stock badge, and an item that is close to its minimum is flagged reorder-soon. Crossing the minimum raises a warning in the dashboard and writes it to the audit log, so the change is on record.

Can I stop taking orders for a dish that has run out?

Yes. Each dish has an availability toggle tied to its stock item. Switch it off and customers can no longer order it from the QR menu; the kitchen and POS see the same state.
